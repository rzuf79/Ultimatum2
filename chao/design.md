## Game title: **Chrono-Splat: The Omelet of Forever**

A deliberately unstable science-fantasy RPG about time travel, bad prophecy, snack-based cosmology, suspicious castles, obsolete computers, planetary tourism, and one extremely vindictive cosmic breakfast chef.

The structure is clearly inspired by **Ultima II**: five time-period overworlds, time gates linking them, Earth-like maps that keep changing by era, visits to the Solar System, absurd towns and castles, and a final villain hiding in a mythic “outside time” zone. Ultima II itself used five Earth time periods plus space travel to the planets, with many towns/castles that were strange, pop-culture-heavy, and not always plot-relevant. ([shrines.rpgclassics.com][1])

This version keeps the same high-level count:

* **5 time-period overworld maps**
* **9 planetary/offworld maps**
* **17 named enterable places**
* **4 time gates per time period**
* Same “why is this here?” energy: castles next to diners, medieval rail stations, moon accountants, wizard supermarkets, time doors in swimming pools, and so on.

---

# Backstory

Long ago, the world was kept stable by **The Panclock**, a divine machine that chopped causality into digestible hours. Every dawn, the Panclock rang, the sun rose, history advanced, and breakfast remained breakfast.

Then came **Professor Yolkayne**, royal chronomancer, failed pastry judge, and inventor of the **Causality Griddle**. He believed history was too linear, too dignified, and insufficiently seasoned. His first experiment folded yesterday into tomorrow and created a sentient omelet that screamed the name of every future king.

The royal court banished Yolkayne to the **Minute Before Creation**, a mythic non-era where unfinished legends are stored. Unfortunately, he found the cracked shell of the Panclock there and learned how to scramble time.

Now the ages overlap. Dinosaurs remember mortgages. Medieval knights queue at airports. Post-apocalyptic mutants worship fax machines. The moon has declared itself a dentist.

Yolkayne has hidden the **Twelve Brass Eggshells** across history and space. Once assembled, they will form the **Omelet of Forever**, a reality-dish capable of frying all timelines into one eternal, rubbery breakfast.

The player is **The Errant Utensil**, a nobody chosen by the broken Panclock because they were “nearby and holding a fork.”

# Time periods

## 1. **Crustacea Prima**

The impossible prehistory before continents learned manners.

Landscape: one massive supercontinent shaped vaguely like a fried egg, with inland seas, volcanoes, giant shellfish, and stone vending machines.

### Locations

1. **Castle Ladlekeep**
   A half-built castle made of coral, bone, and kitchen tiles.
   Key NPCs:

   * **Lord Apron**, who heals the player by shouting encouraging menu items.
   * **Dame Clatter**, who claims armor was invented by clams.
   * **The Wall Fish**, a talking mounted fish that has not yet been caught.

2. **Sauropod-on-Sea**
   A prehistoric trade town where dinosaurs sell saddles, pies, and fake fossils.
   Key NPCs:

   * **Bronto Bill**, transport dealer.
   * **Maggie the Mud Oracle**, who speaks only in weather forecasts.
   * **A Small Accountant**, who is somehow from the future.

3. **The Clam Seminary**
   A monastery of shellfish mystics.
   Key NPCs:

   * **Abbot Bivalve**, who teaches basic magic.
   * **Sister Spoon**, keeper of the first Brass Eggshell clue.

### Time gates

* North volcanic gate → **Neon Middling**
* West lagoon gate → **The Apology War**
* East fern gate → **Brass Afternoon**
* South tar-pit gate → **Minute Before Creation**

---

## 2. **Brass Afternoon**

A bogus ancient-medieval era where empires, crusaders, poets, and door-to-door salesmen all coexist.

Landscape: broken kingdoms, bronze towers, wandering tax collectors, suspiciously modern hotels, and rivers shaped like cursive letters.

### Locations

4. **Fort Picklebaron**
   The main “kingly” castle, though the king is just a very confident tailor.
   Key NPCs:

   * **Baron Pickle**, who sells titles and dubious prophecies.
   * **Sir Hemlock of Receipts**, who explains taxation as combat.
   * **The Green Knight’s Intern**, who drops a gate clue.

5. **Market of the Nine Almosts**
   A town full of shops that nearly sell what you need.
   Key NPCs:

   * **Santrella**, weapon seller and retired pirate queen.
   * **Old Man Conveyor**, who sells ships if you bring him a blue ribbon.
   * **Priest Xerox**, who copies blessings incorrectly.

6. **Hotel Umbrella**
   A luxury inn that exists centuries before umbrellas are invented.
   Key NPCs:

   * **The Concierge of Doom**, who improves player attributes for a fee.
   * **Bellhop 7**, who is clearly a robot but denies it.
   * **The Oracle in Room 404**, who gives intentionally over-specific hints.

### Time gates

* Castle courtyard gate → **Neon Middling**
* Desert sundial gate → **Crustacea Prima**
* Mountain toll gate → **Minute Before Creation**
* River gate → **The Apology War**

---

## 3. **Neon Middling**

The “modern” era: malls, rockets, diners, detectives, bureaucracy, and monsters in parking lots.

Landscape: Earth-like geography, but cities are in the wrong places and the oceans contain escalators.

### Locations

7. **New Snackramento**
   A city of weapons, armor, aircraft, vending machines, and aggressive civic pride.
   Key NPCs:

   * **Mayor Coupon**, who grants permits to steal aircraft.
   * **Zed McLaser**, sells advanced weapons.
   * **Officer Maybe**, who arrests people randomly but politely.

8. **Port Folio**
   A harbor city, spell market, and food depot run by marine lawyers.
   Key NPCs:

   * **Captain Ledger**, teaches ship navigation.
   * **Wanda of the Wharf**, sells spells and sandwiches.
   * **The Oracle Buoy**, who floats indoors.

9. **Castle Kindly-Bent**
   A royal healing center in the middle of a suburban golf course.
   Key NPCs:

   * **Queen Kettle**, heals the player for donations.
   * **Mister Tapwater**, a fake sage with one useful fact.
   * **The Goblin Caddy**, who knows where the rocket key is buried.

### Time gates

* Airport gate → **The Apology War**
* Golf pond gate → **Brass Afternoon**
* Shopping mall gate → **Crustacea Prima**
* Rooftop gate → **Minute Before Creation**

---

## 4. **The Apology War**

The ruined future after every nation apologized at once and nobody accepted.

Landscape: radioactive apologies, broken skyscrapers, mutant debate clubs, oceans filled with melted plastic trophies.

### Locations

10. **Pirate Customer Service**
    A raider harbor where all pirates speak in corporate support scripts.
    Key NPCs:

* **Captain Holdmusic**, sells access to the space shuttle.
* **Peg-Leg Pamphlet**, gives the clue to Planet Blivv.
* **The Refund Oracle**, answers questions after a 45-turn delay.

### Time gates

* Crater gate → **Neon Middling**
* Broken bridge gate → **Minute Before Creation**
* Toxic forest gate → **Brass Afternoon**
* Submerged gate → **Crustacea Prima**

---

## 5. **Minute Before Creation**

The mythic age outside time, where discarded legends, unbuilt castles, and unused boss monsters wait for purpose.

Landscape: floating islands, black seas, storybook ruins, staircases to nowhere, and punctuation marks as terrain.

### Locations

11. **The Unwritten Citadel**
    Yolkayne’s final stronghold. It is not reachable safely until late game.
    Key NPCs:

* **Professor Yolkayne**, final villain.
* **The Draft Dragon**, optional clue monster.
* **Princess Semicolon**, imprisoned but deeply annoyed by rescue narratives.

### Time gates

* Left gate → **Crustacea Prima**
* Second-left gate → **Brass Afternoon**
* Second-right gate → **Neon Middling**
* Right gate → **The Apology War**

# Offworld maps

The player eventually acquires a stolen rocket, then travels to nine absurd planets/moons. Some have major locations; some are deliberately empty or nearly useless, preserving the “why did I come here?” feel.

## 6. **Mercury: The Toasted Coin**

No named settlement.
Terrain: burning plains, tiny craters, useless signposts.

Purpose: contains a hidden landing clue written in scorch marks.

---

## 7. **Venus: The Perfume Swamp**

No named settlement.
Terrain: pink fog, carnivorous flowers, perfume storms.

Purpose: optional treasure and a joke dungeon entrance that loops back to the start.

---

## 8. **Mars: The Red Bureau**

### Location

12. **Town of Marjoram**
    A dusty frontier town run by retired robots and embarrassed cowboys.
    Key NPCs:

* **Sheriff Hex**, sells heavy weapons.
* **Molly Marrow**, armor merchant.
* **The Rusted Barber**, knows the location of the first space Eggshell.

---

## 9. **Jupiter: The Floating Schoolyard**

### Location

13. **Village of the Prefects**
    A town of rich children, cloud monks, and disciplinary balloons.
    Key NPCs:

* **Headboy Nimbus**, demands lunch money as tribute.
* **Cloudmother Vera**, sells healing food.
* **Tiny Professor Blimp**, gives the Password of Aeration.

---

## 10. **Saturn: The Ring Road**

No named settlement.
Terrain: circular roads, toll booths, lost minotaurs in traffic cones.

Purpose: a navigational trap; needed only to learn that not every planet matters.

---

## 11. **Uranus: The Sideways Carnival**

### Location

14. **New Jape City**
    An amusement park city tilted ninety degrees.
    Key NPCs:

* **Jester Probate**, sells spells in exchange for jokes.
* **Madame Upside**, tells the player which Eggshell is fake.
* **The Ferris Oracle**, whose prophecy changes every rotation.

---

## 12. **Neptune: The Computer Aquarium**

### Location

15. **Camp Bitbubble**
    A computer camp under an ocean of liquid math.
    Key NPCs:

* **Counselor BASIC**, teaches decoding spells.
* **Kid Kernel**, explains rocket coordinates.
* **The Debug Clam**, reveals Yolkayne’s weakness.

---

## 13. **Pluto: The Forgotten Suburb**

### Locations

16. **Tommorowville**
    A tiny suburb of people who insist Pluto is still important.
    Key NPCs:

* **Mayor Almost**, gives a false but useful quest.
* **Grandma Frostbyte**, sells rare food.
* **The Snowman Barrister**, knows where the Brass Ladle is.

17. **Town of Muckler**
    A weapons-and-armor settlement built entirely inside one enormous mitten.
    Key NPCs:

* **Muckler Smith**, sells the best armor.
* **Two-Socks Nan**, sells the best weapon.
* **The Cold Oracle**, gives the final route to Blivv.

---

## 14. **Planet Blivv: The Extra Planet Nobody Admits Exists**

### Locations

18. **Castle Crumbelune**
    A healing castle orbiting a fake moon.
    Key NPCs:

* **Father Ladle**, grants the Blessing of Nonstick.
* **The Choir of Spoons**, sings the melody needed to open Yolkayne’s gate.

19. **Town Basko-Basko**
    A smug little town where everyone is a shopkeeper or spy.
    Key NPCs:

* **Basko Prime**, sells the Mirror Skillet.
* **Basko Minor**, lies about the Mirror Skillet.
* **The Clerk of Coordinates**, reveals the path to the Minute Before Creation.

Note: This gives the design one more named offworld place than Ultima II’s visible list. To keep the **same playable-place count**, make **Castle Crumbelune and Town Basko-Basko share one enterable combined map**, separated by a wall and locked doors. They count mechanically as one “place map” but appear narratively as two places. That preserves the strange old-RPG feel of two unrelated things crammed into one map.

# Main quest: complete plot sequence

## Act I — The Fork is Chosen

1. **Begin in Neon Middling, outside New Snackramento.**
   The player has basic gear, no clear goal, and a message from the broken Panclock:
   “THE OMELET APPROACHES. FIND BREAKFAST BEFORE BREAKFAST FINDS YOU.”

2. **Visit Castle Kindly-Bent.**
   Queen Kettle explains that Professor Yolkayne is corrupting history from the Minute Before Creation. She cannot send the player there directly because “the drawbridge has not been invented yet.”

3. **Go to New Snackramento.**
   The player learns three practical goals:

   * get a ship,
   * get access to a rocket,
   * find the Brass Eggshells.

4. **Grind, explore, and obtain the Blue Ribbon.**
   Pirates begin appearing after the player reaches sufficient notoriety. Defeating pirate captains can yield a **Blue Ribbon**, which allows the player to command ships.

5. **Sail to Port Folio.**
   Captain Ledger teaches that the time gates are not random; they form a loop around Yolkayne’s “breakfast logic.” The Oracle Buoy says:
   “THE FIRST SHELL IS BEFORE TEETH. THE SECOND IS WHERE HOTELS LIE.”

---

## Act II — The First Eggshells

6. **Use the mall gate to reach Crustacea Prima.**
   The player travels to the prehistoric supercontinent.

7. **Visit the Clam Seminary.**
   Abbot Bivalve gives the first real clue: Yolkayne shattered the Panclock into twelve pieces, but only **six true Brass Eggshells** are needed. The other six are decoys that cause instant hiccups and mild timeline damage.

8. **Recover the First Brass Eggshell from the Tar-Pit Shrine.**
   This requires crossing a dinosaur-infested bog and defeating **The Proto-Chicken**, a boss that technically should not exist yet.

9. **Travel to Brass Afternoon.**
   In Hotel Umbrella, the Concierge of Doom improves the player’s attributes for large payments. The Oracle in Room 404 says the next Eggshell is “on a world that forgot it was small.”

10. **Acquire the Brass Key from Fort Picklebaron.**
    Baron Pickle will only surrender it if the player brings him a future object: the **Disposable Crown**, bought from a vending machine in Neon Middling.

---

## Act III — The Rocket and the Ridiculous Solar System

11. **Return to Neon Middling and steal or buy an aircraft permit.**
    Mayor Coupon “legalizes” theft by issuing the player a **Temporary Heroic Misappropriation License**.

12. **Use aircraft to reach the sealed rocket field.**
    The Goblin Caddy at Castle Kindly-Bent reveals that the rocket key is buried under the ninth hole. Digging there gives the **Ignition Crouton**.

13. **Launch into space.**
    The player can now visit the nine offworld maps. Most are strange, optional, or misleading.

14. **Visit Mars: Town of Marjoram.**
    The Rusted Barber identifies one true Eggshell and warns that Venus contains only “perfumed disappointment.”

15. **Visit Jupiter: Village of the Prefects.**
    Headboy Nimbus demands a toll. The player can pay, fight, or use a joke learned from New Jape City. The reward is the **Password of Aeration**, needed for Neptune.

16. **Visit Neptune: Camp Bitbubble.**
    Counselor BASIC decodes the Panclock’s damaged message. The player learns Yolkayne cannot be harmed unless struck with something that “reflects breakfast back at the cook.”

17. **Visit Pluto: Tommorowville and Town of Muckler.**
    Grandma Frostbyte and Muckler Smith help the player assemble late-game gear:

* **Muckler Mail**
* **Two-Socks Blade**
* **Snowproof Sandwiches**
* clue to Planet Blivv

18. **Visit Planet Blivv.**
    At Castle Crumbelune, Father Ladle gives the **Blessing of Nonstick**.
    At Basko-Basko, the player acquires the **Mirror Skillet**, the only weapon that can reflect Yolkayne’s time magic.

---

## Act IV — Sorting the True from the Fake

19. **Return to Uranus: New Jape City.**
    Madame Upside identifies which Eggshells are false. The player must now have:

* First Brass Eggshell from Crustacea Prima
* Second Brass Eggshell from Brass Afternoon
* Third Brass Eggshell from Mars
* Fourth Brass Eggshell from Jupiter
* Fifth Brass Eggshell from Pluto
* Sixth Brass Eggshell from Planet Blivv

20. **Ignore Mercury, Venus, and Saturn unless desired.**
    They contain optional treasure, misleading messages, and enemies, but no required quest item. This preserves the old “why is this planet even here?” texture.

21. **Bring the six true Eggshells to Port Folio.**
    The Oracle Buoy fuses them into the **Half-Omelet Compass**, which does not point north. It points toward “the most narratively irresponsible place nearby.”

22. **Use the Half-Omelet Compass at any time gate.**
    It unlocks the hidden fifth destination: **Minute Before Creation**.

---

## Act V — The Minute Before Creation

23. **Enter the Minute Before Creation.**
    The world is abstract, broken, and hostile. Terrain includes punctuation reefs, unfinished NPCs, and monsters called **Retcons**.

24. **Visit the Unwritten Citadel.**
    The front gate refuses entry unless the player has:

* Mirror Skillet
* Blessing of Nonstick
* Half-Omelet Compass
* at least one deliberately useless item, proving the player has “understood history”

25. **Rescue Princess Semicolon.**
    She is not the love interest. She is the citadel’s punctuation lock. Freeing her allows the player to pass through incomplete sentences.

26. **Confront the Draft Dragon.**
    Optional but useful. If defeated, it reveals that Yolkayne’s spell has three layers:

* the Panclock shell,
* the Causality Griddle,
* Yolkayne’s ego.

27. **Reach the Kitchen at the End of Maybe.**
    Yolkayne begins cooking the Omelet of Forever. Each turn, he pulls monsters from random eras: dinosaur knights, robot clams, pirate accountants, moon dentists.

28. **Use the Mirror Skillet.**
    Regular attacks only damage Yolkayne’s body. The Mirror Skillet reflects his “Scramble Time” spell, cracking the Causality Griddle.

29. **Use the six Brass Eggshells.**
    They restore the Panclock’s rhythm. The battlefield begins cycling through all five time periods every few turns.

30. **Final choice: kill Yolkayne or spare him.**

**If killed:**
Time stabilizes, but breakfast becomes permanently illegal in one minor kingdom.

**If spared:**
Yolkayne is sentenced to run the Panclock’s cafeteria. The ending is happier, but all future clocks smell faintly of onions.

31. **Return to Castle Kindly-Bent.**
    Queen Kettle declares the player **Utensil Regent of Tuesday**. The Panclock rings. History resumes, mostly in order.

# Intended tone and design rules

**The world should feel inconsistent on purpose.** The maps should not explain themselves too neatly. A castle can be next to a mall. A prehistoric monk can know rocket coordinates. A moon town can have a pub, oracle, and transport shop for no good reason.

**But the plot is more readable than the original model.** The player always has a next major objective:

1. Learn about Yolkayne.
2. Get ship access.
3. Use time gates.
4. Find early Eggshells.
5. Get aircraft and rocket access.
6. Visit planets.
7. Identify true Eggshells.
8. Get Mirror Skillet and Blessing of Nonstick.
9. Enter the Minute Before Creation.
10. Defeat Yolkayne.

**The joke logic should be consistent even when the world is not.** Everything ridiculous should still serve one of three purposes: clue, gate, resource, or memorable nonsense.

**Minor NPCS**
All towns are populated with denizens offering bits and pieces of lore, describing mainly the place/era they are located in and rarely the whole game world.
